Template ↗

Design a simple visual or text-based flow that shows how your BeReal feature works: from the moment a user meets it to the point they complete the main action, and what happens if things go wrong.

👋👆✅🔁

It doesn't need to be beautiful. It just needs to clearly show the logic and journey of the experience.

You'll bring together everything from the last two trainings:

The core happy path (the user's main journey).
The two critical states (first-time vs. repeat; success vs. error).
The microcopy that makes it feel authentic and human.
1

Copy the Template

Make a copy of the Flow Map template and rename it "Flow Map: [Feature Name] – [Your Name]".

Make a copy ↗
✅You're on track if your copy is renamed and opens on Before You Map.
2

Review your PRD-lite and user stories.

In your doc: Before You Map

Re-read your founding hypothesis, problem, target user, and user stories. Paste your founding hypothesis and ask yourself:

What's the one main goal of this feature?
What's the simplest way for the user to reach it?
What does "done" look like?
⭐ Where's your key moment: the one screen that would tell you whether your founding hypothesis is right?
3

List your essential screens or steps.

In your doc: Screen List

Keep it to 4–6 key moments. Example:

ScreenIntro / Permission
PurposeIntroduce the feature and get opt-in
ScreenMain Action (Prompt or Post)
PurposeUser performs the core behavior
ScreenReaction or Confirmation
PurposeFeedback: success or error message
ScreenRepeat Loop
PurposeOptional: how the user comes back the next day
ScreenSettings / Opt-Out
PurposeUser control (turn off or adjust the feature)

Each screen should have a single purpose. If one does too much, split it.

4

Sketch or storyboard your flow, but write it in words first.

Here's a trap designers know well. Lenny Rachitsky shared an idea from designer Bob Baxley, the "primal mark": as soon as you draw your first sketch, "everything you do will be a response to that."

🔒

John Zeratsky agrees: once something looks real, it's easy to get locked into it.
