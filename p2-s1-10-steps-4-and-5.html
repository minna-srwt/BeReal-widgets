Step 5: Interpret and Prioritize

12345

Now turn the data into insight. Ask yourself:

?What are competitors doing that users love, and why?
?Which of those features fit BeReal's goals?
?Where could we innovate instead of imitate?

April's trick here is to take every feature and ask: "So what?" Why would a customer actually care? Here's how that played out for Help Scout:

FeatureChat only appears when a real person can reply.
→
So what? (the value)Customers feel treated like people, which builds loyalty.
→
Who cares most?Online brands whose customer service is their main relationship with customers.

Not every business cares about that value. A giant phone company with a huge call center might mostly want to cut costs.

But an online brand that sells straight to its customers, with no stores and no salespeople, has customer service as nearly its only conversation with them. For those businesses, Help Scout's difference matters a lot.

That's how Help Scout pitches itself: competitors treat customer service as a cost to cut, while Help Scout treats it as a way to grow. It doesn't have to beat Zendesk at everything. It has to be clearly better for the customers who care most.
