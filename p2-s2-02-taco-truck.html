Then you look around and realize: you're not alone.

To stand out, you first have to map your competition. Here are the three main types you'll face:

Direct competitors

Businesses offering the same product or services in the same niche or target market.

Indirect competitors

Businesses solving the same problem with a different product or service but compete for the same customers.

Replacement competitors

Also called substitute competitors, they offer an alternative product that customers might use instead of yours.

Direct Competitors

You
Taco Bell

Across the street sits Taco Bell, serving tacos too. Maybe not as fresh as yours, but familiar and fast. You're both after the same customers craving tacos. Every taco they sell could have been yours.

That's a direct competitor: someone solving the same problem, for the same people, in almost the same way.

Indirect Competitors

You
Burger truck

Down the block, you notice a burger truck. Different food, same goal: feeding hungry people fast. That's indirect competition.

An indirect competitor serves a similar need, but in a different way. For the lunch crowd, tacos and burgers both scratch the same itch.

2

Indirect Competitors

Examples:
Netflixvs.YouTube(competing for screen time, not the same product)
Calmvs.Spotify(both compete for your relaxation time)

Indirect competition isn't always zero-sum. People might use both, depending on their mood.
