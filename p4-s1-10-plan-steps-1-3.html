Design a clear, 1-page Focus Group Plan that sets out exactly how you'll test your BeReal feature with peers: who you'll invite, what you'll test, and how the session will flow.

By the end, you'll have a concise, professional plan, similar to what product managers use before a moderated testing session.

Define your objective

In your doc: Objective

Write a simple, 2–3 sentence goal statement that answers: "What do I want to learn from testing this prototype?" Example:

The goal of this focus group is to understand whether the new feature feels authentic to BeReal users, is easy to navigate, and encourages daily engagement without creating pressure.

Paste your founding hypothesis, then write your goal statement and your "isn't working if" line in Objective. Check it against the Blobular vs. Pointed table.

2

Define your objective (continued)

Then add one more line, the one that keeps you honest. Remember Judd Antin's rule: we don't validate, we falsify. Look back at your founding hypothesis from Project 3 and finish this sentence:

"My feature isn't working if  ."For example: "My feature isn't working if people can't explain what the joint notification does, or say they'd turn it off."
✅You're on track if a participant's honest reaction could actually make your "isn't working" sentence come true.
😵‍💫If nothing anyone says could prove you wrong, your goal is too vague. Make it more specific.
3

Decide who to invite

In your doc: Participants

Plan for 5–10 participants. Ideal participants:

  • A mix of active and casual BeReal users (or people who use social photo apps)
  • Comfortable speaking in small groups
  • Ideally in the age group you're designing for (often 18–25 for BeReal)

If possible, include 1–2 people who don't use BeReal often. Fresh eyes can spot what regulars miss.

Example mix: 3 regular BeReal users, 2 occasional users, 2 non-users (who use Instagram/Snapchat).
