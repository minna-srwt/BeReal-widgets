What Good Looks Like

📌 These examples are a Focus Group Plan for Spotify: Friday Fresh, the feature from the Project 3 examples. The details and numbers are made up for illustration. Notice what the strong one does: it's built so the session could prove it wrong.

📏 The rubric

A goal that could prove you wrong · The test question

Could a participant's honest reaction make your "isn't working" line come true?

0 · Not yetNo goal, or no "isn't working if" line
1 · Getting thereA goal and a line, but the goal is blobular, or nothing anyone says could prove it wrong
2 · StrongA pointed goal tied to build, refine or pivot, and an "isn't working" line from your founding hypothesis that a real reaction could prove
The right people in the room · The test question

Will you hear from more than one kind of user?

0 · Not yetFewer than 5 people, or all one kind
1 · Getting there5–10 people, but no non-users, or mostly close friends
2 · Strong5–10 people mixing regular, occasional and non-users, with a reason for each group
A session that runs to plan · The test question

Could someone else run your session from this page?

0 · Not yetStages or timings missing
1 · Getting thereAll 5 stages timed, but outside 30–45 minutes, or a tool missing
2 · Strong5 stages adding up to 30–45 minutes, people explore the demo on their own, and every tool is ready

✅ Strong example

2 · 2 · 2
Objective
Founding hypothesisIf we help Daily Mix listeners whose mix goes stale by the end of the week solve repetitive listening with Friday Fresh, we believe they'll choose it over putting on a YouTube video in the background because it refreshes their own mix without any searching.
Objective
GoalTo find out whether listeners notice the new songs in their Friday mix without being told, and whether they'd keep playing instead of switching to something else. This tells me whether to build Friday Fresh as is, refine how the new songs are shown, or pivot.
Objective
My feature isn't working ifmost participants can't tell what changed in their Friday mix, or say they'd skip the new songs and turn it off.
Participants · 7 total
3 · Regular Daily Mix listeners (study or work playlists most weekdays)The people the hypothesis is about
2 · Occasional Spotify usersWill they notice the change without a habit to compare it to?
2 · Non-users (use YouTube or Apple Music)Fresh eyes: is it clear without knowing Daily Mix?
Format & Duration · Session Flow
Format & DurationVirtual via Google Meet, 40 minutes. Participants open the wireframe link on their own laptops.
1. Introduction · 5 min"We're testing a new Spotify feature. No right or wrong answers. We're testing the feature, not you."
2. Spotify Experience Warm-Up · 5 min"What do you put on when you study?" "When do you skip a song?"
Session Flow (continued)
3. Prototype Demo · 10 min"Click through as you normally would." I stay quiet for the first 2 minutes.
4. Discussion · 15 minOpen questions from my discussion guide
5. Quick Survey · 5 minShare the Google Form link
Total40 min
Pitfall · Tools
Pitfall and fixI'll want to explain the "New this Friday" labels. Fix: let them find them, then ask "What do you think this means?"
Tools✅ wireframe link tested · ✅ discussion guide (Step 2) · ✅ Google Form (Step 3) · ✅ notes doc · ✅ recording only with everyone's OK · ✅ a classmate taking notes
How it scores
A goal that could prove you wrong: 2 The goal is pointed (notice the songs, keep playing) and names the decision. The "isn't working" line comes straight from the hypothesis, and two reactions in one session could make it true.
The right people in the room: 2 Seven people across three groups, each with a reason, including two who don't use Spotify.
How it scores
A session that runs to plan: 2 Five stages adding up to 40 minutes, a quiet demo, and every tool ready, with recording only by consent.

Why it works: the plan is built so the session could prove Friday Fresh wrong.

How it scores

The difference in one line: the strong plan is built so the session could prove Friday Fresh wrong; the not-yet one is built to hear "yeah, it's cool."
