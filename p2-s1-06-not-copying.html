But here's the part people get wrong: benchmarking isn't copying.

One of the best examples comes from someone who has built several of the most viral apps of the last decade.

Nikita Bier co-founded tbh, an app for high schoolers that he sold to Facebook for over $30 million, and later Gas, which he sold to Discord. On Lenny's Podcast, he described how tbh started.

tbh → FacebookGas → Discord
😍

A high schooler told him about a game teens were playing on Snapchat: post your story, and friends reply with emojis saying what they like about you.

#1

Around the same time, Nikita noticed the #1 app in the entire US App Store was Sarahah, an app for sending anonymous messages, where people can say something to you without revealing who they are. And the whole app was in Arabic.

To Nikita, that was a huge signal. If American teens were pushing through an app they couldn't even read, they really wanted what it offered.

So he studied it. He read the reviews and the posts about it, and found the catch:

lots of users were getting cruel, bullying messages.

What tbh built

So tbh kept what users loved and removed what hurt. Instead of typing anything they wanted, people answered positive polls, like "Who has the best smile?"

That's the real point of comparing. Nikita didn't copy Sarahah. He studied why people wanted it and where it was failing them.

In April Dunford's terms, Sarahah was exactly what tbh had to beat.
