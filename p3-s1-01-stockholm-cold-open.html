In January 2009, a designer named Jake Knapp flew to Stockholm for a week.

Stockholm

He worked at Google, on the Gmail team. But this trip was about something else: a side project a few colleagues had been stuck on for years.

The idea was video calls that worked right inside your web browser. No downloads, no special software.

It was going nowhere.

And as Knapp tells it, the whole Stockholm office looked like it might be shut down.

The team had already tried everything you're supposed to try:

the perfect pitch,
a detailed document spelling out exactly what the product should do,
the ideal design.

None of it convinced Google's leaders, Larry Page, Sergey Brin, and Eric Schmidt, to back it.

"Perfection is not working."

— Jake Knapp, on that moment in Stockholm

So for one week, they cleared their calendars and did something completely different.

Google Hangouts→Google Meet

By Knapp's account, it was the most productive week of his career. The project went on to launch as Google Hangouts, and it eventually grew into Google Meet, the video-call tool millions of people use for school and work today.
