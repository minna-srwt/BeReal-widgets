1234Use Likert scales for sentiment and usability

A Likert scale is a rating question with a short range of labeled answers, usually 1 to 5. It's one of the most common ways PMs measure feedback. You can use it for:

  • Ease of use → "How easy or difficult was it to understand the feature?"
  • Visual clarity → "How visually appealing did you find the screens?"
  • Value → "How useful do you think this feature would be for you?"
  • Emotional fit → "How authentic does this feature feel to BeReal's tone?"

Example response scale:

1Strongly Disagree
2Disagree
3Neutral
4Agree
5Strongly Agree

Why five labeled points? Judd Antin, who led research at Airbnb, says survey scientists have found that people answer less precisely once a scale goes past about five to seven options, and that unlabeled numbers make it worse.

That's his big problem with the famous 0–10 "How likely are you to recommend this?" question (called NPS). He says it "makes all the mistakes," and adds: "I am not a person who goes around recommending operating systems." People don't recommend most things, so the question measures something odd.

So keep your scales short and labeled, and be careful with "would you recommend…" questions.

1234Include at least one behavioral question

These connect directly to BeReal's MAU goal (Monthly Active Users). Example:

"How likely are you to open BeReal more often because of this feature?"
Scale: 1 (Not at all likely) → 5 (Very likely)

Behavioral questions help you judge whether your idea could actually bring people back.

1234Add 1–2 open-ended questions

Numbers are great, but people often say the most valuable things in their own words. Example prompts:

"What did you enjoy most about this feature?""What, if anything, would you change or improve?""Describe how this feature made you feel."
