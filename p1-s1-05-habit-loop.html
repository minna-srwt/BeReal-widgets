Once infinite scroll took over, everything else became a tool to keep users hooked.

The habit loop is simple and powerful: trigger, action, reward.

This is also why the loop never really finishes. Laudi points out that "recurring revenue businesses cannot think about growth as ending at acquisition" — the same logic applies to an ad-supported app. Getting you to download isn't the finish line; every session after that is still being optimized, because your continued attention is the thing being sold. That's why the loop keeps escalating instead of settling once you're a regular user.

Each reward gives your brain a small burst of dopamine. Over time, your brain starts expecting the next hit, which makes the loop harder to break.

(It's the same principle slot machines use: reward you irregularly so you keep trying.)

♥♥♥

This cycle brings classic signs of addiction:

Tolerance (needing more engagement for the same satisfaction).

Withdrawal (uneasy when you can't check).

Conflict (arguments or guilt around use).

Relapse (falling back into heavy use after a break).
