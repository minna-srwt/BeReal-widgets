Think of yourself as a guide, not a presenter.

Your responsibilities:

✅Keep the discussion on track and on time
✅Encourage everyone to take part
✅Stay neutral and non-defensive
✅Capture real reactions, not polite ones
✅Make participants feel heard

Remember: you're testing the prototype, not the people. If something confuses users, that's useful information, not a failure.

🙂🔍
Set the tone at the start

How you begin decides how open people will be.

Think back to LogMeIn. When the team finally asked users why they hadn't downloaded the software, Sean Ellis says they deliberately avoided anything that felt "creepy" or formal. They made it look like a friendly note from customer service: "What happened?" And people answered honestly.

Your opening works the same way. Start friendly and informal.

nameemailreason
"What happened?"

Sample opening script:

"Hey everyone, thanks for joining!I'm testing an early BeReal feature idea.It's not finished, and there are no right or wrong answers.I'm just curious about your honest reactions.Please speak freely, even if something feels confusing or weird.That helps me learn."

Then cover these basics:

🔒Say the session is confidential: no answers will be quoted with names.
⏺️Ask permission to record (if you're recording).
📝Let them know you might take notes or ask follow-up questions.
📱Emphasize: "We're testing the feature, not you."

Keep everyone engaged

In a small group, participation can easily become uneven: some people talk a lot, others stay quiet. Your job is to gently balance the room.

✋

Tap each situation

"That's a great point, thank you. I want to make sure others can share too. Does anyone see it differently?"

"We haven't heard from you yet. What do you think when you see this screen?"

"Interesting. Why do you all feel that way?"
"Is there anyone who feels differently?"

Different opinions = better insights.

Keep the session on track

Your total time is 30–45 minutes, so structure matters. Watch the clock. If one topic drifts too long, sum it up and move on:

"That's really helpful. I'll note that and keep us moving so we cover everything."

Flow reminder:

1. Intro / Warm-Up2. Current BeReal habits3. Prototype testing4. Discussion (Interest → Usability → Value)5. Quick wrap-up + survey

Always end on time. Respecting people's time builds trust.
