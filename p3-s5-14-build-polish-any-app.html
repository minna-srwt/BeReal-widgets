Polish your screen designs

Jake Knapp describes a solo founder in his current program, building a product called Axion Orbital, who did exactly this:

His first prototype was "pretty bare bones," because in that first round he was focused on getting the message right.
Once that was clear, he used AI tools to build a second one: "a much more real-looking" version, very detailed.

His second prototype: "a much more real-looking" version, very detailed.

You're at that second stage now: your message is clear, so it's time to make it look right. Use short, specific follow-ups:

"Make all buttons the same size and shape, with rounded corners."

"Even out the spacing so every screen has the same margins."

🧠Starting point, not the end-point. If everyone outsources their thinking to AI, everyone ends up thinking about the same thing.

Style guide reminders (BeReal tone):

Colors: white, light gray, and one muted accent (soft yellow or soft blue)
Fonts: a clean sans-serif (e.g., Inter, Open Sans, or Poppins)
No gradients or heavy shadows. BeReal's design is flat, calm, and real.

Run the any-app test

BeRealCover up the word "BeReal" on your screens: could this be an Instagram or Snapchat feature?

If so, it's gone generic. Remember John Zeratsky's rule, don't outsource the thinking, and go back to your founding hypothesis. What makes your feature something only BeReal would do? Make that part unmistakable.
