Step 1: Choose the Right Competitors

12345

Start by identifying who you're comparing against. Not every name in the industry, just the ones that matter most.

PMs typically select:

Direct competitors: products that solve the same problem for the same audience
Indirect competitors: products that meet the same need in a different way
Here's what that looked like for Help Scout:
Zendesk, and the many other customer-support tools
A plain email inbox, or a basic shared inbox the team already uses

That second column is easy to skip. But April says Help Scout often isn't replacing Zendesk at all. It's replacing just email.

Hold onto that. It turns out to matter a lot more than most teams expect.

Step 2: Decide What You'll Compare

12345

This is where you define your "benchmark categories." For product managers, these often include:

Core product features: what the product actually does (e.g., posting, filters, notifications)
User experience (UX): how easy or enjoyable it is to use
Performance metrics: speed, engagement, retention
Market performance: downloads, ratings, social media buzz
Pricing and positioning: how it's marketed and how it makes money

April adds one sharp filter: compare the things you have that the alternatives don't. Anything every competitor can already do won't help you win.

For Help Scout, that list included:

A shared inbox built for customer service, so no customer's message gets missed or answered twice
A chat bubble that only appears when a real person is available to reply
Every customer gets a person helping them, not just a ticket number

The goal is to find meaningful comparison points that connect to BeReal's goals, not just a long list.

Step 3: Gather Your Data

12345

Now it's time to collect information. You don't need fancy tools, just curiosity and reliable sources:

📱App stores and websites for features, reviews, and screenshots
📈Tools like Semrush, Similarweb, or Sensor Tower for performance data
💬User feedback on Reddit, X (Twitter), or forums for honest opinions
📰Analyst reports and articles for big-picture trends
