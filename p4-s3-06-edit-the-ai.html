456★Integrating AI + Your Expertise

AI speeds up drafting, but it has never met your participants. Left alone, it writes answer options from its imagination.

Sean Ellis has a better order. When he wants to know what users value, he doesn't write the multiple-choice answers himself:

💬

STEP 1 · Ask openFirst, he asks an open-ended question, like "What is the primary benefit you get?", to collect answers in users' own words.

☑️

STEP 2 · Turn into choices (AI can help here)Then he turns the most common answers into multiple choice: "four distinctive benefit statements" people pick from.

❓

STEP 3 · Ask whyThen he asks "Why is that benefit important to you?"

You can use AI for step 2. After your discussion, paste in the phrases your participants actually used, and ask:

"Here are phrases my focus group used about this feature: [paste]. Turn the most common ideas into 4 clear multiple-choice options for a survey question. Keep their wording as close as possible."

If AI's questions still feel generic, re-prompt with BeReal context:

"Focus on simplicity and social authenticity: real friends sharing unfiltered daily posts, not gamified interaction."

The more specific your prompt, the better the result.

456★Pro Tips
✅Use AI for speed, not for final approval. Review every question yourself.
✅Keep language short, friendly, and human: no jargon or marketing tone.
✅Run a quick test with 1–2 friends first to spot confusing questions.
✅If answers start sounding repetitive, that's good: it means patterns are emerging.
🧠Starting point, not the end-point. If everyone outsources their thinking to AI, everyone ends up thinking about the same thing. Use it to test your own thinking.
