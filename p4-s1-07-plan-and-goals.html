What is a Test Plan?

A test plan is your roadmap for running a research session. It defines:

  • What you want to learn (goals)
  • Who you'll invite (participants)
  • How the session will unfold (flow)
  • How you'll capture results (notes or recordings)

Think of it as your session playbook.

Step 1: Define your session goals

Before you plan logistics, get clear on what you want to learn.

Your test isn't about proving your design works. It's about learning why it does or doesn't. Judd Antin points out that "quick user studies just to validate our assumptions," run at the very end, are usually "too late to matter." Your plan should name what could prove you wrong.

Judd also has a useful test for whether a goal is any good. He describes a lot of research as "middle range": questions like "How do Airbnb hosts feel about their payment options?" Interesting, but, in his words, "not quite pointed enough." The answers are hard to act on.

middle range

A pointed goal connects to a decision you actually have to make. For you, that decision is: build this feature as is, refine it, or pivot?

A pointed goal tells you whether to build, refine, or pivot.

✅ Pro Tip: limit yourself to 2–3 clear goals. If you try to learn everything at once, you'll learn nothing well.
