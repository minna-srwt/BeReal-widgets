Step 1 of 4 · Ask one precise question

"Find data about Duolingo" sends you everywhere.

A precise question tells you what to collect and when to stop:

Are people coming back to Duolingo every day and every month, and why or why not?

Notice it has two halves: how many (numbers) and why (what people say). You need both.

Step 2 of 4

Start with the company's own numbers

Companies that sell shares publicly report results every quarter, usually on an "Investors" page. Duolingo's results for the third quarter of 2024 reported:

37.2 milliondaily active users
113.1 millionmonthly active users

That's a DAU/MAU of about 33%: roughly one in three monthly users opening the app on a given day.

📌 Private companies like BeReal don't have to publish quarterly results. That's why its numbers come from occasional announcements and interviews, and why a second source matters even more.

Step 3 of 4

Look for a second source

A second source only counts if it's independent: it measured or found the number itself, rather than repeating someone else's.

✗ Repeats it: not a second source
✓ Measured on its own: counts
Step 3 of 4

Kinds of second source

Measurement firms

Where to lookSensor Tower, Statista Good forTrends over time, comparing apps Watch out: They're estimates, not exact counts. Some need a free account.

Press

Where to lookNews search for "[app] daily active users" Good forContext, interviews with leaders Watch out: Articles often repeat the company's number, which doesn't make it a second source

AI research tools

Where to lookPerplexity, with citations turned on Good forFinding articles and reports fast Watch out: Never take a number from the AI's answer. Click through to the original source every time.
Step 3 of 4

Good prompts for an AI research tool:

"What are the most recent news articles about Duolingo's daily active users, with citations?"
"Summarize analyst opinions on Duolingo's user retention and engagement, with citations."

Always click through to the original article to verify details. That habit is what separates surface-level research from credible analysis.

Step 4 of 4

Find the "why" in what users say

Numbers tell you how many. Users tell you why. The best free places to hear them:

⭐ App Store and Google Play reviews

How to searchOpen the app's page, then sort by Most recent Tip: Recent reviews describe the app as it is now, not as it was years ago

💬 Reddit

How to searchSearch the app's name, or its own community (Duolingo has r/duolingo) Tip: Look for long posts about daily habits, not just quick complaints
