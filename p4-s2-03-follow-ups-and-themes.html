The best insights often come from the second question you ask. Sean Ellis has a great example.

He was helping an early email tool called Xobni ("inbox" spelled backwards). He asked users: "What is the primary benefit you get?" The answer was useful but flat: it helps me find things faster in my email.

Then he asked a second question: "Why is that benefit important to you?" And the same phrase kept coming back: "I'm drowning in email."

That second answer was the real story. When Sean later used "Drowning in email?" as the hook in his marketing, it worked, because it was in the users' own words.

Not everyone communicates the same way.

  • For quieter groups: ask more visual or hands-on questions ("Which part of this screen draws your eye first?").

Your goal is to balance voices while keeping the discussion natural.
