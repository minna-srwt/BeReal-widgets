What Good Looks Like

📌 These examples are a PRD-lite for Spotify: Friday Fresh, the feature chosen in the Step 2 example. The details and numbers are made up for illustration. Notice what the strong one does: a stranger could picture the same feature, person and number.

📏 The rubric

A bet that holds together · The test question

Are all five blanks specific, and does each one match a section below?

0 · Not yetMissing, or blanks like "users" and "it's better"
1 · Getting thereAll blanks filled, but one is vague, or one doesn't match a section below
2 · StrongEvery blank specific, "what they do today" comes from your Project 2 work, and every blank matches a section below
A definition a stranger could picture · The test question

Could someone new picture the problem, the person and the number?

0 · Not yetA vague wish ("make BeReal more fun"), "everyone" as the user, or no metric
1 · Getting thereMostly specific, but the user is broad, the metric isn't tied to a behavior, or there are no non-goals
2 · StrongA real behavior, a specific person and situation, a measurable metric with 1–2 leading indicators, and real non-goals
Stories you could test · The test question

Could a friend watch a demo and check each criterion?

0 · Not yetStories written as specs, or no criteria
1 · Getting there2–3 stories, but one mixes behaviors, or criteria are vague, or none is an opt-out or guardrail
2 · Strong2–3 stories from the user's side, one behavior each, and 3–5 pass/fail criteria that cover every story, including an opt-out or guardrail

✅ Strong example

2 · 2 · 2
Feature Name: Friday Fresh
Founding hypothesisIf we help Daily Mix listeners whose mix goes stale by the end of the week solve repetitive listening with Friday Fresh, we believe they'll choose it over putting on a YouTube video in the background because it refreshes their own mix without any searching.
The definition
ProblemBy Friday, Daily Mix listeners hear the same songs they heard on Monday, so they skip, give up, and play something else.
Target UserPeople who play Daily Mix most weekdays while studying or working, and who don't want to search for new music.
The definition
MAU Success MetricsPrimary: % of Daily Mix listeners who play it on Friday. Leading: skips per Friday session; Friday sessions per user per month. Expected: +10% Friday plays (my estimate).
The definition
AssumptionsListeners want a few new songs, not a whole new mix. Listening history can pick songs that still fit the mood.
Non-GoalsBuilding a new playlist screen; adding social sharing.
User Stories
1As a Daily Mix listener, I want a few new songs in my mix by Friday, so that it doesn't feel stale.
2As a listener, I want to see which songs are new, so that I know the mix changed.
3As a listener, I want to turn Friday Fresh off, so that I can keep my mix as it is.
Acceptance Criteria
Story 1On Fridays, Daily Mix includes 3 songs the listener has never played.
Story 1The new songs match the mix's mood.
Story 2New songs show a "New this Friday" label.
Acceptance Criteria
Story 3Listeners can turn Friday Fresh off in one tap from the mix screen.
How it scores
A bet that holds together: 2 Every blank is specific, "YouTube in the background" comes from the earlier research, and each blank matches a section below.
A definition a stranger could picture: 2 A real behavior, a specific listener, a Friday metric with two leading indicators, and two real non-goals.
How it scores
Stories you could test: 2 Three stories, one behavior each, and four pass/fail criteria that cover every story, including the opt-out.

Why it works: a stranger could picture the same feature, person and number.
