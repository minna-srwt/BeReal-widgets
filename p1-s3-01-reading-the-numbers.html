First, Two Terms You'll See Everywhere

MAU
DAU
MAU (monthly active users)

How many different people used the app at least once in a month

An app with 100 MAU had 100 different people open it at least once this month

DAU (daily active users)

How many different people used the app on a given day

If 30 of those 100 open it on a typical day, that's 30 DAU

(The numbers in this table are made up, just to show how the terms work.)

100MAU

Put them together and you get one of the most useful numbers in product: DAU ÷ MAU, or how many of your monthly users show up on a given day.

In the example above, that's 30 ÷ 100 = 30%. The higher it is, the more your app is part of people's daily routine.

📌

Notice what that means for you: the daily experience you just mapped is what drives the monthly number BeReal cares about. If a day goes badly, someone stops coming back, and eventually they stop counting as a monthly user.

Posting inside the 2-minute window

Here's What These Numbers Can't Tell You

Every number above came from one kind of source: BeReal itself. That doesn't make them wrong. But it means you should ask the same questions you'd ask about any number:

Who measured it?When?Is it reported or estimated?Does a second source agree?

These four questions are your credibility check. You'll use them on every number you find in this step.

And there's one more thing BeReal's numbers can't tell you: why. They show how much people engage, not why they stay, or why they leave. For that, you need to look somewhere else.
