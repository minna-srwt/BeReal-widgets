PMs usually structure focus group discussions around three kinds of questions:

Tap each pillar

Interest → Does the idea grab attention or feel relevant?

"Would you want to try this feature?"
"What's your first impression when you see it?"

Usability → Is it easy to understand and use?

"How would you describe what's happening on this screen?"
"Was there anything confusing or unclear?"

Impact → Does it change behavior or emotion?

"Would this make you open BeReal more often?"
"Does it make the app feel more real, or less?"

These three pillars (Interest, Usability, Impact) form the foundation of your test plan and discussion guide.

Example scenario (BeReal context)

Say your feature is "Daily Duo." You might show your prototype and ask:

"What do you think is happening on this screen?""Would this feel natural inside BeReal?""How do you feel when you see both friends' posts together?""Would this feature make you post more often?"

The discussion that follows will reveal subtle truths (hesitation, excitement, skepticism) that no survey could capture alone.

😬🤩🤨
Best practices for your focus group
✅Keep it small and conversational (5–10 people).🪑
✅Stay neutral: don't defend or explain your design.🤐
✅Notice what people show, not just what they say (facial expressions, tone).👀
✅Ask why after every answer.❓
✅End with a quick survey to put numbers to what you heard.📋

Here's a warning worth taking seriously. Judd Antin led research at Airbnb, twice, and later ran its whole design studio. He's seen a lot of focus groups, and he's blunt about them: when people run them mainly to feel close to customers, he says they're "97% performance", "well-intentioned, but it isn't focused on learning."

"We don't validate, we falsify. We are looking to be wrong."
