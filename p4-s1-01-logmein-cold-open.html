About 20,000 of them signed up every day.

But then something strange happened. Only about 2,000 actually downloaded the software. The other 18,000 signed up and walked away.

So the team did what data-driven teams do. They ran experiments.

They ran more than 10 A/B tests, trying different versions of the download page to see which one would get more people through.

Show two versions to two groups. Keep whichever works better.

An A/B test is a way of comparing two versions of something by showing each one to a different group of people and seeing which one performs better. For example, BeReal could test two different wordings on its "post now" button and keep the one that gets more people to post.

The numbers would tell them which version won. And at LogMeIn, none of them won. Ten tests in, nothing had moved.

Finally, someone on the team asked a question so obvious nobody had thought of it:

"Why don't we just ask them?"

So they did. They sent the people who'd signed up but never downloaded a simple, friendly note asking what happened.

The answers that came back were not what anyone expected. And once the team understood them, their very next test improved downloads by 300%.
