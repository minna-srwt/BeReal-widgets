That's called triangulation: checking whether different kinds of source agree.

The same complaint showing up in reviews, on Reddit, and in your own teardown starts to look like something real.

Gia describes this exact moment in her own research:

"It can literally be a numbers game… if you take… 100 or so survey responses, you can break that down like here's the thing they said they cared about most."

The Pattern Rule

For each theme you find, count how many different kinds of source show it:

What you seeWhat it isWhat to do
✓ in 3 or more kinds of source
🟢 Pattern
Treat it as real. It can carry your argument.
✓ in 1–2 kinds of source
🟡 Signal
Worth watching, not yet proof. Say so.
Sources disagree
🔴 Conflict
Write down which source you trust more, and why.

Two things that trip people up:

= 1 kind
Ten reviews are still one kind of source. A pattern needs different kinds (reviews, Reddit, numbers, your teardown), not more of the same kind.
A conflict isn't a failure. If a company says engagement is up while recent reviews complain, that's interesting. Maybe the complainers are a loud minority, or maybe the company's number is old. Your job is to say which you believe, and why.

Then Ask: So What?

Once you know your patterns, go back to your teardown:

Does the data back up, complicate, or contradict your broken moment?

Any of the three is a good answer. Changing your mind because of evidence is exactly what PMs are supposed to do.
