Once you've chosen your top-scoring idea, it's time to define it clearly.

A Product Requirements Document (PRD) is the bridge between a creative idea and a buildable feature. For real product managers, a PRD communicates:

What we're buildingWho it's forWhy it mattersWhat success looks like

In this step, you'll write a PRD-lite: short, focused, and actionable. It's not a corporate document. It's a clarity exercise.

What is a PRD-lite?

Think of it as a one-page story that explains:

1The problem you're solving
2The target user you're solving it for
3The success metric (how you'll measure impact)
4The boundaries: what it won't do
5The assumptions behind your logic

Why bother writing it down? Jake Knapp puts it this way: every new product has a founding hypothesis, a core bet about who it's for and why it'll work. But

"it's just usually not explicit. It's usually hidden, and different people on the team may have different ideas about what it is."

A PRD-lite drags that hidden bet into the open, where everyone can see it, question it, and agree on it.
