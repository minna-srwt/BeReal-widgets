That's where she found it: sheets of paper taped to the wall in a row, mapping out an Airbnb customer's entire journey — a host, a guest, start to finish.

📌

Internally, the team called it Project Snow White, after the Walt Disney biography that inspired it: Disney's team had to invent the storyboard just to make Snow White work, because the film was too complex to hold in anyone's head at once. Airbnb hired an actual Pixar storyboard artist to draw the keyframes of a customer's trip the same way.

What stopped Gia in her tracks wasn't the craft.

The wall didn't track signups or conversion rates — it tracked what a real person felt at each step, including the parts of their life that had nothing to do with Airbnb at all.

Every milestone was legible at a glance: what the customer was trying to do, and what they needed to feel to keep going.

It was the complete opposite of the funnel thinking she already found gross. This was value to the customer, mapped in full view — not value to the business, buried in a spreadsheet.

She went back to her own office and, with her team, spent two days locked in a room building their own version. That wall is a big part of why Forget the Funnel exists at all.
