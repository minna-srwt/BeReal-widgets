The role of good questions in product testing

Remember LogMeIn, from the start of this project? A flood of new visitors, 20,000 sign-ups a day, but 18,000 of them never downloaded the software. The team ran more than 10 A/B tests. Nothing worked.

Then someone asked: "Why don't we just ask them?"

So they sent the people who'd signed up but never downloaded a friendly note: "Hey, notice you haven't had a chance to use the product yet. What happened?"

"This seemed too good to be true. I didn't believe this was free."

Here's what came back, as Sean Ellis tells it:

The software was free, and people didn't trust it. No A/B test could have discovered that, because nobody had thought to test for it. Once the team understood the real problem, their very next test improved downloads by 300%.

Sean passes on a piece of advice he heard from a data scientist named Oleg Yakubenkov: "it often comes down to asking the right question at the right time."

300%

When product managers test early prototypes, they're not looking for compliments. They're looking for truths. The right questions help you discover:

Clarity: do users understand what your feature does?
Value: do they see why it's worth using?
Emotion: how does it make them feel?
Behavior: would they actually use it again?

In short:

Good questions reveal insights. Bad questions confirm biases.
